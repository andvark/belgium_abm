## 2. Structural modelling assumptions

- **Households are built first, then persons** (assumed). Each simulated household is drawn from a candidate pool generated per (region, household template). IPU re-weights the pool to the person-cell (region x sex x age band) and household-template targets, so the result matches published margins only to the extent the pool supports them; unsupported constraints are reported.
- **Candidate member ages follow the region's age-sex structure** (assumed). Heads and other adults are drawn from the adult part of the regional age-sex pmf; a partner's age is the head's plus a rounded normal difference (SD = `couple_age_difference_sd`); a child's age must satisfy the configured minimum/maximum parent-age-at-birth bounds against every parent. These rules only propose candidates -- they are NOT estimates of Belgian partnership or fertility behaviour.
- **Single-year ages inside a published band are uniform** (assumed). `within_band_age_distribution = uniform` (the only option implemented). Published bands (e.g. 5-year) hide the true within-band shape.
- **Household types and relationships** (assumed). Five household types with declared adult/child counts. Couples are modelled as head + partner (sexes drawn independently of each other, so same-sex couples occur by construction); in OTHER_MULTI households the relationship between adults is not represented; children are linked to the head (and partner, if any).
- **Education, labour state and arrangement are allocated independently of household context** (assumed). Allocation uses the published conditional shares within region x age band (x education): systematic rounding per cell, then a deterministic count-space hill-climb that moves units between categories/cells (cell sizes fixed) so both the cells and their aggregated margins stay within about one unit of expectation. Sex is not a dimension of these tables; intra-household correlation (e.g. partners' education) is absent. Only the published margins are targeted; unpublished joint distributions are not.
- **Work arrangement applies to EMPLOYED persons only** (assumed). EMPLOYED includes employees, sole proprietors and BV owner-managers. The BV share cannot be observed from labour-force tables alone (see `statbel_enterprise_demography`).
- **Persons below working_age_min get no education / labour assignment** (assumed). `education_level = NOT_ASSESSED`, `work_state` and `student_status` are null, `primary_work_arrangement = NONE`.
- **Weights are uniform** (assumed). Every simulated person represents the same number of real persons (target total / simulated total); households likewise.
- **Integerisation** (assumed). Weights are integerised by systematic rounding followed by within-template swap hill-climbing on a chi-square-like objective. Household counts per template are preserved; person-cell counts are near, not exactly equal to, the targets.
- **Timing convention** (assumed). Ages increment at the start of each model year before any transition (locked by tests); no dynamics are implemented yet.
- **Calibration tolerances** (assumed). Warn/fail thresholds and small-cell rules in the configuration are choices, not empirical findings.

## 3. Not implemented / not calibrated

- **Income, wealth, pensions, firms** (not_implemented). Schemas exist; all values are left null. Seeding from EU-SILC/HFCS needs data that are not acquired.
- **Tax and benefit rules** (not_implemented). `PolicyConfig` is an empty skeleton. No statutory rate is stored anywhere; they must come from verified sources (never from an LLM).
- **Demographic transitions (mortality, fertility, partnership, separation, migration)** (not_calibrated). No transition probabilities exist in the code.
- **Education and labour-market transitions** (not_calibrated). No transition probabilities exist in the code.
- **Entrepreneurship entry/exit, firm dynamics, hiring** (not_calibrated). Not implemented.
- **Consumption, saving, housing purchase, credit constraints** (not_calibrated). Not implemented.
- **Fiscal ledgers in a simulation loop** (not_implemented). `FiscalLedger` / `InKindLedger` are unit-tested building blocks only.
- **Seed-to-seed uncertainty and sensitivity analysis** (not_implemented). Reports describe a single realisation.

## 4. Known data gaps (spec section 8)

- Cross-dataset joint distributions (e.g. education x labour state x household type).
- Person-to-BV beneficial ownership and retained-profit attribution.
- Actual lifetime pension trajectories.
- Correlated familial (intergenerational) wealth.
- Firm-household matching.
- Legal tax-policy complexity by tax year and region.
- Longitudinal behavioural parameters (transition hazards).
