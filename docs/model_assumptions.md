> **Status: all phases implemented; calibration is partial.** Real 2025 Belgian aggregates drive the
> population (see `docs/data_real.md`); the lifecycle economy, behaviour and the tax-benefit file are
> assumptions or an illustrative placeholder. No Belgian tax rate, demographic frequency, transition
> probability or income / wealth value is hard-coded or fabricated in the code: every number is read
> from a provenance-tagged data or configuration file. **No claim about real Belgian policy effects is made.**

## 2. Structural modelling assumptions

- **Households are built first, then persons** (assumed). Each simulated household is drawn from a candidate pool generated per (region, household template). IPU re-weights the pool to the person-cell (region x sex x age band) and household-template targets, so the result matches published margins only to the extent the pool supports them; unsupported constraints are reported.
- **Candidate member ages follow the region's age-sex structure** (assumed). Heads and other adults are drawn from the adult part of the regional age-sex pmf; a partner's age is the head's plus a rounded normal difference (SD = `couple_age_difference_sd`); a child's age must satisfy the configured minimum/maximum parent-age-at-birth bounds against every parent. These rules only propose candidates -- they are NOT estimates of Belgian partnership or fertility behaviour.
- **Single-year ages inside a published band are uniform** (assumed). `within_band_age_distribution = uniform` (the only option implemented). Published bands (e.g. 5-year) hide the true within-band shape.
- **Household types and relationships** (assumed). Five household types with declared adult/child counts. Couples are modelled as head + partner (sexes drawn independently of each other, so same-sex couples occur by construction); in OTHER_MULTI households the relationship between adults is not represented; children are linked to the head (and partner, if any).
- **Education, labour state and arrangement are allocated independently of household context** (assumed). Allocation uses the published conditional shares within region x age band (x education): systematic rounding per cell, then a deterministic count-space hill-climb that moves units between categories/cells (cell sizes fixed) so both the cells and their aggregated margins stay within about one unit of expectation. Sex is not a dimension of these tables; intra-household correlation (e.g. partners' education) is absent. Only the published margins are targeted; unpublished joint distributions are not.
- **Work arrangement applies to EMPLOYED persons only** (assumed). EMPLOYED includes employees, sole proprietors and BV owner-managers. The BV share cannot be observed from labour-force tables alone (see `statbel_enterprise_demography`).
- **Persons below working_age_min get no education / labour assignment** (assumed). `education_level = NOT_ASSESSED`, `work_state` and `student_status` are null, `primary_work_arrangement = NONE`.
- **Weights are uniform** (assumed). Every simulated person represents the same number of real persons (target total / simulated total); households likewise.
- **Integerisation** (assumed). Weights are integerised by systematic rounding followed by within-template swap hill-climbing on a chi-square-like objective. Household counts per template are preserved; person-cell counts are near, not exactly equal to, the targets.
- **Timing convention** (assumed). Ages increment at the start of each model year before any transition (locked by tests); the annual schedule is demography -> education -> work and firms -> incomes and fiscal flows -> consumption, saving and assets -> measure. Income measures describe the year's flows; wealth measures are opening (start-of-year, after transitions) balances.
- **Calibration tolerances** (assumed). Warn/fail thresholds and small-cell rules in the configuration are choices, not empirical findings.

## 3. Dynamic model (Phases 2-4): what is observed, derived, assumed

- **Mortality and fertility** (observed). Annual death probabilities (Belgian life table qx by sex and age) and age-specific fertility per woman come from Eurostat (`demo_mlifetable`, `demo_frate`), reference year = baseline year - 1. Fertility does not depend on partnership, education or income; no migration; the population therefore ages and slowly shrinks (a modelling choice, not a projection). Sex of newborns uses `male_birth_share` (assumed 0.5).
- **Education pathway** (derived). Everyone enters school at 15 with LOW attainment; at 18 a share `1 - LOW_25_34` completes upper secondary and a share `HIGH_25_34 / (1 - LOW_25_34)` of those continues to tertiary (graduating HIGH at 22), both derived per region from the observed 25-34 education shares so cohorts reproduce the cross-section. Household wealth rank shifts the odds with an assumed logit slope (`education_wealth_sensitivity`). Dropout and repetition are absent.
- **Labour-market flows** (derived). Job loss, labour-force exit (`job_separation_rate`, `labour_force_exit_rate`) are assumed; job-finding and labour-force re-entry probabilities are derived per region x age band x education so the observed 2025 cross-section is stationary (f = s E/U, g = l E/I, capped). Longitudinal LFS transition data (restricted) would replace them. Inactive persons below 65 are INACTIVE (no early-retirement state).
- **Self-employment dynamics** (assumed). Exit rates are assumed; entry hazards are derived from the age profile of the observed shares so a cohort keeps the cross-sectional profile. Entry odds rise with household wealth rank (`entry_wealth_sensitivity`) and high education, and require start-up money (`business_entry_cost_sole_prop`, `bv_capital_contribution`) from household financial assets (explicit credit constraint). The sole-proprietor / BV split rests on a rough proxy from VAT-enterprise legal-form counts.
- **Household formation and dissolution** (assumed). Partnering (age-assortative matching within region), separation (assets split, children stay with the head), leaving home, orphan adoption and inheritance (estates of households with no surviving adult go to adult children, else a random household of the region) use assumed rates; no divorce law, no inheritance tax, no remarriage differentials.
- **Earnings and productivity** (assumed). Log earnings = education base + age polynomial + persistent skill + AR(1) residual, scaled by a single level factor chosen so the simulated median equivalised disposable income equals the published EU-SILC median (the only calibration). Self-employed owners receive the employee-equivalent labour cost as value added (`*_productivity_multiplier` = 1: comparable productivity), with their own AR(1) business shock.
- **BV mechanics** (assumed). Surplus after accounting cost is split into director pay (`bv_director_pay_share`, taxed as earned income with self-employed contributions), corporate tax, dividends (`bv_payout_ratio`, final withholding tax) and retained earnings (firm equity, counted once in owners' net worth). Negative equity triggers an insolvency event with limited liability; retirement or voluntary exit liquidates the firm as a taxed dividend.
- **Wealth, housing and consumption** (assumed). Initial financial assets, housing and mortgages follow stylised age-income profiles (HFCS microdata are restricted); retired households are seeded from a share of their career-average earnings (`retiree_permanent_income_share`) rather than their pension income. Consumption is a subsistence floor plus a marginal propensity out of disposable income, with a drawdown for retired households; borrowing is constrained and unpaid obligations become explicit `other_debt` credit events; renters can buy a home with a down payment. Baseline wealth is compared with the published ECB HFCS 2023 aggregates for Belgium (`validation_wealth_vs_hfcs.csv`, validation only, not like-for-like): inequality measures and the age profile of older households are close, the median is below, and the wealth of young households is too low.
- **Tax-benefit system** (scenario). All fiscal rules come from a versioned policy YAML. The shipped `configs/policy_illustrative.yaml` is an ILLUSTRATIVE placeholder (every value `scenario`, `verified: false`, banner on every output): it is **not** Belgian law. Statutory rates, thresholds, regional child benefits and pension rules must be supplied from verified sources before any Belgian result is quoted.
- **Distributional validation** (observed). At baseline the simulated Gini, at-risk-of-poverty rate, S80/S20 and income-quintile shares are compared with published EU-SILC indicators (`validation_vs_published.csv`). Only the median is calibrated. The simulation overstates the lower tail (see report) largely because labour status is independent across household members in the population generator.

## 4. Not implemented / still missing

- **Verified Belgian tax-benefit rules** (missing_data). Phase 2 engine and scenario machinery exist, but the policy file is illustrative. Needs verified statutory values (and BELMOD/EUROMOD benchmarks for validation).
- **In-kind benefits (schooling, healthcare)** (not_implemented). `InKindLedger` exists as a tested building block; no in-kind values are attributed in the simulation.
- **Migration, regional mobility** (not_implemented). Population is closed apart from births and deaths.
- **Behavioural response of labour supply, fertility, savings to policy** (not_implemented). Only the explicit scenario knobs act; the fixed-behaviour (static) and re-run (dynamic) results differ only through the channels modelled (accrual, liquidation, firm insolvency, assets).
- **Tax incidence** (not_implemented). Legal payer and economic beneficiary are kept separate; employer contributions are a firm cash flow and no pass-through to wages is assumed.
- **Wealth and tail calibration (HFCS, top-1%)** (missing_data). Needs restricted HFCS microdata or published HFCS tables; with ~1% of persons in the top percentile, tail results need many replicates and are sensitive.
- **Sensitivity analysis over parameters** (not_implemented). Seed-to-seed uncertainty bands are produced by `--replicates`; one-at-a-time parameter sensitivity is available through scenario `economy_overrides` but no automated ranking exists.
- **Monthly cash flow, sector/wage feedback, endogenous rents, macro coupling** (not_implemented). Spec phase 5.

## 5. Known data gaps (spec section 8)

- Cross-dataset joint distributions (e.g. education x labour state x household type).
- Person-to-BV beneficial ownership and retained-profit attribution.
- Actual lifetime pension trajectories (accrual here is a stylised career-average rule).
- Correlated familial (intergenerational) wealth.
- Firm-household matching.
- Legal tax-policy complexity by tax year and region.
- Longitudinal behavioural parameters (transition hazards).
